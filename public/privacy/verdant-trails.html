Privacy Policy for Verdant Trails

App: Verdant Trails (Android; package com.minormending.verdanttrails)
Publisher: Standard Mending Co. (Kevin Ramdath)
Contact: kkramdath@gmail.com
Effective date: 3 November 2026

The short version

Verdant Trails is a walking game. With your permission it reads your step count (and, if you turn it on, your wheelchair pushes) on your phone, and uses it only to move the game. Your steps never leave your device: we never receive them, and they are never sold, shared or used for advertising. The app has no accounts, no ads, no analytics, no tracking and no in-app purchases, and we never receive your name, email, location or game data. The only thing the app does on the internet is check for game updates (new content, fixes) from Expo's update service. That check carries a random installation identifier that is not linked to you. We do not sell or share personal information.

Steps and health data

  • What the app reads. With your permission, the app reads your step counts from Health Connect (Android's health data store, which the phone itself, a watch or another fitness app may write steps into) or, if you choose it instead, from your phone's step sensor. If you turn on wheelchair pushes (Options > Steps, off by default), it also reads your wheelchair push counts from Health Connect, and each push counts as steps in the game. It reads nothing else from Health Connect: no heart rate, sleep, location, exercise routes, weight or any other health data. It never writes anything to Health Connect.
  • How it reads them. Health Connect is read only while the app is open. The step sensor keeps its own running count in the phone's hardware; to turn that count into daily totals, the app notes the sensor's reading on your device when you open the app, when the home-screen widget refreshes, and from time to time in the background (a task Android runs about every half hour or less often). Each note is a number and a time, kept in the app's private storage (the most recent 500 notes).
  • What the steps are used for. Only to play the game: moving your party along the trail, growing your plants, counting down seed pods, your daily goal, streaks and the stats page (including a distance estimate from the stride length you set). Nothing else.
  • Where they are kept. Only on your device, in the app's private storage, as daily totals for the last 120 days plus a lifetime total. The individual step records in Health Connect are not copied.
  • Never sent off the device. Your step and push data is never sent to us or to anyone else, never sold, never shared, never used for advertising or marketing, and never used to decide credit, insurance, employment or anything similar. It is not used to train any model and no person reads it. The use of information received from Health Connect adheres to the Health Connect Permissions policy, including its Limited Use requirements.
  • Activity recognition permission. If you choose the phone's step sensor, Android asks you to allow "physical activity" (activity recognition). The app uses it only to read the step counter. It does not detect or record what you are doing (walking, driving, cycling) and does not use your location.
  • How to stop. You can revoke the permissions at any time: in Health Connect (its app permissions page for Verdant Trails) or in your phone's settings (Apps > Verdant Trails > Permissions). The game then stops reading new steps. Options > Delete save removes the stored daily totals and everything else the game keeps, and uninstalling the app removes all of it.

What else the app stores on your device

  • Your save. Your player name, chosen look, plants, items, trail progress, journal pages, goals, streaks, settings and the step totals described above are saved in the app's private storage on your device so the game can continue where you left off. This data stays on your device; the update check described below does not include it. You can erase it at any time with Options > Delete save, or by clearing the app's storage or uninstalling the app.
  • Your player name is whatever you type at the start of the game (up to 10 characters). It is used only inside the game and stays in your save.
  • The home-screen widget. The Pocket Pal widget needs a small summary on your device: your buddy plant's name and picture, today's steps against your goal, a short line of game news, and the pats you give it. The app writes this summary to its private storage and the widget reads it from there. If you use the phone's step sensor, the widget may read the sensor on the device to show an approximate step count. Nothing in it goes to any server. Options > Delete save clears it too.
  • Device backups. Your phone's operating system may include an app's private data in its own backup (for example Android's backup to your Google account, if you have it switched on). That is handled by your device and its maker under your device settings and their policies, not by Verdant Trails. Turning device backup off in your settings, or deleting the save in the app, stops or removes it.

Notifications

Verdant Trails can show reminders: you reached your daily goal, an evening nudge if you are under half of your goal (at most three times a week), and a warning that a streak ends at midnight. They are local notifications: the app schedules them on your device, using times it works out from your own save and settings, and your phone shows them when the time comes. No notification server is involved and nothing is sent to you or from you over the internet. The app asks for notification permission at the end of the first-run introduction, after explaining why, and you can change or revoke it at any time in the app's Options (one switch for each kind) or in your phone's settings.

Network use and links

  • App updates (the only network use of the app itself). Verdant Trails can fetch small update bundles (the game's content, code and art) so that new content and fixes reach you without a new version from the store. It checks when the app starts and when you return to it, and downloads an update in the background if there is one. The service that provides them is Expo's update service (EAS Update, at u.expo.dev, with the file hosts it points to), run by Expo. Each check or download is an ordinary HTTPS request. It carries:
    • the platform (Android);
    • the app's runtime version (an identifier of the app's native build, not of you) and the update channel it follows;
    • the identifiers of the update that is running, the one built into the app, and any recent updates that failed to start;
    • if the app failed to start last time, a short technical error message about that failure, so that a bad update can be rolled back;
    • a random installation identifier (see below); and
    • what any internet request reveals: your IP address and the device's user agent.

    It contains no name, email, advertising ID, location, contacts, notification token, step or push counts, or other game data (your save is never sent), and we do not use it to identify or track you.

  • The installation identifier. The update software creates a random identifier (a UUID) the first time it runs and keeps it in the app's private storage for as long as the app stays installed. Expo uses it to count how many installations receive an update and to keep the update service working. It is not derived from your device, your accounts or your advertising ID, and it is not linked to your name or any other detail about you; we cannot tell who it belongs to. Uninstalling the app removes it, and a new one is created if you install again. (If Android's own device backup restores the app's data to a phone, the identifier may be restored with it. Clearing the app's storage removes it.)
  • Expo is our service provider. Expo receives these requests on our behalf, only to deliver updates to the app. We do not use the identifier for advertising or analytics and we do not give it to anyone else. How Expo handles the requests it receives is described in Expo's privacy policy: https://expo.dev/privacy.
  • No other network use. The app contacts no other server: no account system, no advertising or analytics code, no crash-reporting service, and no push-notification service. Health Connect and the step sensor are on your phone; reading them uses no network. The game's art, fonts, sounds, plants and story are on your device, and you can play offline. Updates to the app itself (a new store version) are delivered by Google Play.
  • Links you tap. Options > About has links to send feedback (it opens your email app with a draft addressed to us, which you write and send yourself; under a line the draft lists the app version, the update it is running, your chapter and your phone's model and Android version, to help us fix problems, and you can delete any of it before sending; it never includes your steps), to rate the app on Google Play, and to this policy. Each plant fact in the Field Herbarium shows its source, which opens that web page in your browser. If Health Connect needs installing or updating, the app can open its Google Play page. All of these open in your phone's email app, the Play Store or your browser only if you tap them. Those are separate services with their own privacy practices.

If we add another feature that uses the network, we will update this policy before that version is released.

Permissions

The app asks your phone for only what the features above need:

  • Health Connect: read steps (and read wheelchair pushes, only if you turn pushes on), to move the game.
  • Physical activity (activity recognition), only if you choose the phone's step sensor, to read the step counter.
  • Notifications, for the reminders above.
  • Vibration, for haptic feedback (which you can switch off in Options).
  • Run at startup and background work, so that reminders, the widget and the step sensor's notes keep working after your phone restarts.
  • Internet, for the update check above.

It does not ask for your location (precise, approximate or in the background), contacts, camera, microphone, photos, files, usage access, access to other apps or advertising ID, and it never asks to write health data.

Children

The game's content is mild (cartoon plant contests, no blood, no chat, no user-to-user contact) and it asks for no personal information from anyone. Because it reads health data, it is designed for teens and adults and is not directed to children under 13 (see the store listing for the age range it is aimed at). The one identifier it sends, the random installation identifier used by the update check, is not linked to a person and is not used for advertising, profiling or tracking. If you are a parent and have a question, or believe a child under 13 has used the app, write to kkramdath@gmail.com. To remove a child's data from the device, use Options > Delete save, revoke the app's Health Connect access, or uninstall the app.

Your choices and rights

We hold no account, name, email, step data or game data about you, so there is nothing of that kind to access, correct or export from us. Your save and your step totals are yours, on your device: revoking the permissions stops new reads, and Options > Delete save or uninstalling removes what is stored. The random installation identifier is stored on your device and removed when you uninstall the app (or clear its storage); it is not linked to you, so we cannot look it up for a request, and any copy of the update requests that Expo keeps is governed by Expo's privacy policy. If you do not want the app to contact the update service, you can uninstall it, or use it with the internet turned off for the app in your phone's settings; the game itself works offline. For any privacy question, write to kkramdath@gmail.com.

Changes to this policy
