# Characters

All names are working titles.

## The player

A **junior botanist**, newly hired at the Fallowfield Herbarium. Name and look
are chosen by the player, and they're referred to as they/them in docs. Their
skill is noticing things. Their reason to travel starts as "catalogue the
Quickened" and grows into "stop Rootstock."

## Dr. Imogen Vale: mentor

Director of the Fallowfield Herbarium. A field botanist who'd rather be
outside. She was the first to notice the Quickened in her own greenhouse, and
she gives the player their starter and the **Field Herbarium** (the Pokédex
equivalent). She's warm, scatterbrained and fiercely protective of wild
plants.

She was once a colleague of Rootstock's leader, which she admits late in Act 2.

## Bram Thorne: rival

The son of Rootstock's leader, though he doesn't know what his father is doing
at first. He steals one of the three starter seedlings from the Herbarium the
night after the Quickening.

- **Early:** treats his plant as a tool and battles to win.
- **Middle:** losses and the player's example crack that.
- **Late:** finds out his father caused the Quickening and turns on Rootstock.
  He shows up to help at the Elder Grove.
- His starter evolves the "hard" way (forced growth) until he learns to tend
  it. That's a story beat with a visible sprite payoff.

## Rootstock: the villains

*In grafting, the rootstock is the base plant that every other variety is
fixed onto. Everything above it depends on it.*

- **Mercer Thorne**, the leader. An agronomist whose family farm was wiped out
  by blight that spread through a single-variety crop. His conclusion was the
  wrong one: wild variety is chaos, and the fix is *control*. He wants to graft
  the Elder Grove onto a rootstock he controls, so that every Quickened plant
  in the region answers to one root. He's sympathetic, sure he's right, and
  heartbroken underneath.
- **The grunts** are farmhands and lab techs in grey work coats with
  graft-tape armbands. They use **graft collars** to force Quickened plants to
  obey (a cruel shortcut, and a mechanic: see 06).
- **Three admins**, each a dark mirror of a Conservatory head:
  - **Shears**: pruning and cutting.
  - **Dr. Calloway**: forced growth, the person behind the Bloom Lake event.
  - **Wren**: the network engineer who runs the Root Relay takeover.

pressed specimen of their signature plant, mounted on a herbarium card.

## The Council (Elite Four) and the Keeper (Champion)

| Seat | Name | Type | Signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apothecary | **Belladonna** | Bloom + Ghost (→ Poison) | Foxglove, nightshade, oleander |
| Sleeper | **Mimi Osa** | Bloom + Wood (→ Psychic) | Sensitive plant (folds at a touch), prayer plant (folds its leaves at night) |
| Rotter | **Titus Arum** | Bug + Ghost (→ Dark) | Corpse flower, dead-horse arum (both pollinated by carrion flies) |
| Kindler | **Pyra** | Fire | Chili pepper, fireweed, flame lily |
| **The Keeper** | **Rowan Vale** | Mixed | Dr. Vale's estranged sibling, who has guarded the Elder Grove gate for 20 years |

Council seats are themed by plant lore, not by a single type. With the
9-type chart (decision Q5), each seat fields core types. The arrow shows the
type that seat moves to once Poison, Psychic and Dark are added.

## Supporting cast

- **Old Fennimore**, the retired botanist on the Hedgerow route. He hands over
  the strange seed in Chapter 1 (the "Mr. Pokémon" egg errand). It turns out
  to be a Centuryheart seed.
- **The Nursery Garden couple** in Glasshouse City. They handle pollination
  and seeds (the Day Care).
- **Greenhouse keepers** in each town (the Pokémon Centre nurses). All of
  them are named after herbs.
- **Pip**, a kid in every town who has a fact about the local plants.
  Optional, and a delivery vehicle for real botany trivia.
