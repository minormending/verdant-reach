# Mechanics, explained in-world

Each Crystal system gets an in-world reason. Where Crystal's version is
iconic trade dress, ours is deliberately different (the pod is not a red and
white ball).

| System | Crystal | Here | In-world reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Creatures | Pokémon | **Quickened** | Woken by the Elder's alarm |
| Capture | Poké Ball | **Terrarium pod**: a clear glass, acorn-shaped capsule | A tiny portable greenhouse. A weakened or curious plant *chooses* to root in it. |
| Dex | Pokédex | **Field Herbarium** | A botanist's journal. "Seen" = sketched; "caught" = a pressed leaf sample. Every entry holds one true fact. |
| Fainting | Fainted | **Wilted** | Losing a contest drains water. Wilted plants recover. |
| Healing | Pokémon Centre | **Greenhouse** | Water, light and warm soil, with an herb-named keeper |
| Badges | Badges | **Pressed Marks** | Mounted specimens from each Conservatory |
| Levels | Level | **Vigour** | How established the plant is |
| Evolution | Level / stone / trade | **Growth** | Real life-stage changes, triggered as shown below |
| Breeding / eggs | Day Care / eggs | **Nursery Garden / seeds** | Two compatible plants are pollinated and produce a seed; the player sows it and it sprouts. |
| Day / night | Day and night cycle | **Photoperiod** | Sun-lovers are stronger by day, night bloomers by night. Some species only appear in one. |
| Shiny | Shiny | **Sport** | Horticulturists' real word for a natural mutation, such as a variegated leaf |

## Growth triggers (evolution)

| Trigger | Item / condition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Vigour (level) | — | Most lines |
| Sunlight | Grows only in daytime | Sunflower bud → bloom |
| Moonlight | Grows only at night | Moonflower vine → night bloom |
| Cross-pollination | **Trading** | Lines that "need a partner", in place of trade evolutions |
| Tending | High friendship | Oak sapling → Great Oak: "a tree you cared for" |
| Fire | **Ember Ash** item | Fire-adapted pine cones open: Lodgepole cone → seedling |
| Frost | **Cold snap**, an item or route | A bulb that needs winter before it flowers (true of tulips and snowdrops) |
| Graft collar | Rootstock's method | Forces growth early but caps stats. Bram's arc: he removes his collar and his plant regrows. |

## Field moves (HM equivalents)

| Crystal HM | Here | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Cut | **Prune** | Clears brambles |
| Strength | **Uproot** | Roots really can split rock |
| Surf | **Raft** | Ride a giant lily pad |
| Fly | **Seed Glide** | The Javan cucumber's gliding seed |
| Flash | **Glow** | Uses the **Foxfire Lantern** key item: a jar of glowing fungus. Fungi are scenery, not creatures (decision Q7). |
| Whirlpool / Waterfall | **Root Bridge** | Living root bridges are a real thing |

## Move flavour (examples)

Every move describes a real plant behaviour:
- **Allelopathy**: poisons the soil, like black walnut, which makes the
  chemical juglone.
- **Photosynthesise**: heals more in sun.
- **Thorn Jab**.
- **Nectar Lure**: lowers accuracy.
- **Sap Seal**: raises defence.
- **Seed Burst**: like the squirting cucumber, which shoots its seeds out.
- **Snap Trap**.
- **Night Fold**: the prayer plant's evening fold, as a defence boost.
- **Spore Cloud**: only usable by Quickened plants that partner with fungi.

## Wild encounters

The Quickened hide in tall grass, hedges, reeds and canopies, which are places
plants actually grow. Encounters are rustles. Water routes use floating plants
and seagrass. Rare encounters include the Burr Wanderer, which appears behind
the player after they walk through brush.
