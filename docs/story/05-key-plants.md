# Key plants

Every plant listed here is real. Every evolution follows the real life
cycle. Facts marked ✦ are for herbarium entries; check each one before
shipping.

## Starters

Crystal's fire/water/grass triangle doesn't work when everything is a plant.
Proposed replacement: **Wood, Fire and Water**.
- Water beats Fire.
- Fire beats Wood.
- Wood beats Water: roots drink it.

| Type | Line | Stage 1 → 2 → 3 | Why it's a good starter |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wood** | **Oak** | Oak Acorn → Oak Sapling → Great Oak | ✦ A single oak can support hundreds of other species. Proven in the sprite test. |
| **Fire** | **Chili pepper** | Chili Blossom → Green Chili → Red Chili | ✦ Capsaicin burns mammals, but birds can't taste it, so birds spread the seeds. |
| **Water** | **Giant water lily** (*Victoria*) | Lily Seedpod → Lily Pad → Giant Water Lily | ✦ The pads can hold the weight of a small child. ✦ Its flowers open white, then turn pink on the second night. |

Bram steals the starter that's strong against the player's.

## Legendaries

### The Centuryheart: Long Bloom legendary (box art)
- **Real plant:** *Puya raimondii*, Queen of the Andes.
  - ✦ A flower spike up to about 10 m tall with thousands of flowers.
  - ✦ Blooms once after many decades (often quoted as 80 to 150 years), then
    dies.
- **Role:** the bloom that carried the alarm. Its seed is the
  "egg" the player carries all game.
- **The catch:** it can't be caught as an adult; it's dying. The player
  *grows* one from the seed after the credits.

### The Elder: the Grove legendary (climax)
- **Real basis:** Pando, a quaking-aspen colony in Utah.
  - ✦ About 47,000 trunks that are all one organism, sharing one root system.
  - ✦ One of the heaviest living things on Earth.
- **Role:** the network's heart, frightened awake. The encounter happens once
  it's calm.
- **Design:** a single trunk in battle, with "many trunks" animation. Unlike
  anything else in the roster.

### The Three Wanderers: roaming, post-game
These are seed-dispersal legends. Each roams the way its seeds really travel.

| Wanderer | Real plant | How it roams | ✦ Fact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tumbleweed** | Russian thistle (*Salsola*) | Wind, across the desert routes | Breaks off at the root and rolls, scattering seeds as it goes |
| **Coconut** | *Cocos nucifera* | Sea, along the coast and islands | Floats and stays viable across ocean crossings |
| **Burr** | Burdock (*Arctium*) | Hitches rides on travellers (it appears *behind* the player) | Its hooks inspired Velcro |

### Methuselah: the mythical (post-game)
- **Real plant:** Great Basin bristlecone pine. ✦ Individual trees are more
  than 4,800 years old.
- **Role:** time-themed events, the Celebi analogue. It "remembers" the last
  Quickening, the one the folklore songs are about.

## Naming convention

Decision Q4: **real common name plus a stage word.** Species are real plants,
and the names say so.

- **Stage 1 and 2** use a real botanical stage word: *Acorn, Seed, Seedpod,
  Bulb, Sprout, Seedling, Shoot, Bud, Pad, Pup, Fiddlehead, Rosette.*
- **Stage 3** is the plant's plain name, or that name with an honorific
  where the plain name is ambiguous: *Great Oak, Giant Water Lily, Ancient
  Dragon Tree.*
- **Single-stage species** use just the plain name (e.g. *Ghost Pipe*).
- **Legendaries** keep their proper names (*the Centuryheart, the Elder*).
- **Sports** (shinies) are written with the horticulturists' convention:
  *Great Oak 'Variegata'*.

## Roster

Decision Q11: **about 100 species for v1**, which is about 250–300 sprites
across 1–3 stages each. The lines below are a starting seed list.

### Core types (decision Q5)

| Type | Lines (stage 1 → 3) |
|---|---|
| Wood | Oak (starter); bramble (flower → green berry → blackberry); English ivy (cutting → climber → flowering ivy) |
| Fire | Chili (starter); fireweed (seed fluff → shoot → magenta spike), the first plant back after a fire |
| Water | Giant water lily (starter); seagrass; mangrove (floating propagule → seedling → stilt-rooted tree) |
| Bug | Sundew (seedling → rosette → flowering); pitcher plant (sprout → small pitcher → tall pitchers) |
| Bloom | Rose (hip → bud → bloom); tulip (bulb → shoot → flower); sunflower (seedling → bud → bloom), proven in the sprite test |
| Ghost | Ghost pipe (one stalk → nodding → seed head); moonflower (seed → vine → night bloom) |
| Thorn | Prickly pear (pad → padded cactus → fruiting); saguaro (pup → column → armed giant) |
| Frost | Snowdrop (bulb → shoot → flower); moss campion (cushion → mound → flowering) |
| Dragon | Dragon's blood tree (seedling → umbrella sapling → ancient); snapdragon (seed → spike → bloom) |

### Plants for the deferred types

Until those types are added, these use core types. The Council relies on
the first three.

| Later type | Lines | Core type for now |
|---|---|---|
| Poison | Foxglove; nightshade | Bloom / Ghost |
| Psychic | Sensitive plant (folds when touched); prayer plant | Bloom / Wood |
| Dark | Corpse flower (corm → single leaf → titan bloom); dead-horse arum | Bug / Ghost |
| Flying | Dandelion (bud → flower → seed clock); Javan cucumber, whose seeds have about a 13 cm glider wing | Bloom / Wood |
| Rock | Living stones (*Lithops*): pebble → split pair → flowering | Thorn |

Lessons from the sprite tests, for choosing species:
- **Bold, simple silhouettes work best**: gourds, acorns, fiddleheads,
  flower heads.
- **Avoid plants with many small overlapping parts** (dense rosettes, fern
  clumps), or show their stage 3 as one iconic part.
